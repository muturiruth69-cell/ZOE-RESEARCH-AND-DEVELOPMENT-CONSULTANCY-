How We Build

Six stages. Each one has a gate.

We never start a build without a named owner inside your organisation, real data in hand, a written definition of “working”, and a signed data-processing agreement.

Frame

Define the workflow, users, decision and what “working” means numerically.

Gate: signed success definition

Ground

Get real data and documents, assess quality, agree the permission model.

Gate: real data in hand

Build

Assemble retrieval, orchestration, guardrails and interface.

Gate: 50 to 100 case test set exists

Evaluate

Score against the test set, test adversarially, fix, re-score.

Gate: target met, or shortfall reported

Pilot

Real users, real work, monitored, with a rollback path.

Gate: users complete real tasks

Handover

Documentation, admin training, monitoring and support.

Gate: trained, named owner accepts

Risks We Plan For

What usually makes AI projects fail, and what we do about it.

Data reality

“Our data” is 40 spreadsheets

We never quote before seeing a real extract, and price a short data assessment first when needed.

Adoption

It works, but nobody uses it

Adoption is a research problem. Workflow observation and training are in scope, and usage is measured at day 90.

Confident error

Answers invented with total assurance

Everything is grounded in retrieval, cited, instructed to refuse when unsure, tested for it, and the residual rate disclosed.

Scope drift

“Could it also…”

Stage gates, a written scope and a change-request procedure.

Orphaned systems

Nobody to run it after handover

Either a support agreement or a trained, named internal owner. We don’t deploy into an organisation with neither.

Privacy

Personal data in the wrong place

Data-processing agreements, permission models and lawful basis confirmed before anything is ingested.

Our Boundaries

What we will not build.

Refusing the wrong work protects our clients, the people affected by these systems, and the trust our work depends on.

Read our Responsible AI policy
  • Foundation models
  • Autonomous decisions in clinical, legal, credit, hiring or benefits contexts without a qualified partner and a human in the loop
  • Systems on data we have no lawful basis to process
  • Surveillance of individuals, covert employee monitoring, or biometric identification without explicit informed consent
  • Anything that scrapes personal data from social platforms or behind logins
  • Systems whose success cannot be defined and measured
  • Systems nobody will own after handover
  • Deepfake, impersonation or synthetic-identity applications

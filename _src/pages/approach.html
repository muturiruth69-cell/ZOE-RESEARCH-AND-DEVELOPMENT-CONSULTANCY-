The ZOE R&D Cycle

Eight stages. Eight honest gates.

From a problem somebody complains about to a solution that has been tested and shown to work, with a decision point at every step.

The Question That Comes First

“What would you do differently depending on what we find?”

It is the most valuable question in any engagement. If nobody will act differently whatever the answer, research cannot help, and we will say so. Research commissioned to justify a decision already taken is a trap we decline.

Most engagements use a slice of the cycle rather than the whole of it. We tell you which stages your work covers and which it doesn’t, so you always know what the evidence can support.

Stage by Stage

What happens, what you receive, and what must be true to move on.

01

Problem Discovery

What is actually going on here?

A structured framing conversation, stakeholder mapping and a first look at existing data. We ask “what decision does this inform?” until it is answered.

Typically 1 to 5 days

Output

Problem Brief: the problem as stated, the problem as understood, who is affected, what decision depends on it, and what is already known.

Gate 1: Is there a real decision waiting on this?

02

Validation & Framing

Is this the real problem, and is it big enough to solve?

A rapid evidence review, 5 to 10 exploratory interviews, a check on whether the problem has been solved elsewhere, and a first sizing.

Typically 3 to 10 days

Output

Validation Note: the reframed problem, the evidence it exists, who has it and how badly, and the researchable question.

Gate 2: Is it real, large enough, and researchable within budget and time? Sometimes the problem described isn’t the problem you have.

03

Evidence Generation

What is actually true?

Research design, protocol, instruments, ethics and regulatory clearance, sampling, fieldwork, data management, analysis and interpretation. The analysis plan is written before data collection begins. No exceptions.

Typically 3 to 10 weeks

Outputs

Protocol · instruments · consent materials · cleaned, documented dataset · Findings Report · Decision Briefing.

Gate 3: Review passed, data quality checks passed, limitations stated plainly.

04

Solution Design

What could fix this, and which option is best?

Three to six genuinely different options (not one option and two straw men), co-designed with users where possible and scored on desirability, feasibility, viability and impact.

Typically 1 to 3 weeks

Output

Solution Concept: the chosen option, why it beat the alternatives, what it requires, what it costs and what could go wrong.

Gate 4: Which finding forced this choice? If none did, we redesign.

05

Prototype

What is the cheapest thing we can build to find out?

We climb one rung at a time: sketch, paper prototype, clickable mock-up, working pilot with real data, production system. A paper mock-up that fails on day three has saved months.

Typically 1 to 6 weeks

Output

The prototype, plus a written statement of what it is meant to test.

Gate 5: Is there a defined test with a pre-stated pass mark? If not, it’s a demo, not a prototype.

06

Testing with Users

Does it work for the people it’s for, where they actually are?

Usability testing, field pilots, accuracy and performance measurement, safety and failure testing. A system meant for a 3G handset in a maize field is tested there, and by someone who did not build it.

Typically 1 to 4 weeks

Output

Test Report: what was measured, what happened, what failed, what must change.

Gate 6: Was the pass mark met? We report the honest number.

07

Validation & Decision

Should this go live, be changed, or be stopped?

Four independent tests, all of which must pass: technical (reliable and maintainable), user (people will use it without being made to), commercial (value exceeds running cost) and operational (someone owns and resources it).

Outcome

GO · ITERATE · STOP.

Gate 7: A documented STOP is a successful project. “We recommended our client not build this, and told them why” is work we are proud of.

08

Implementation, Scale & Learning

Did it work in the real world?

Deployment, training, adoption monitoring, a mandatory Day-90 review, and outcome measurement against the decision from Stage 1.

Outputs

Handover pack · Day-90 review · outcome measurement · case study · lessons captured for future work.

Gate 8: Has the learning been captured where the next project will find it?

How a Gate Works

Twenty minutes, one form, one decision.

A gate is not bureaucracy. It is what stops a project drifting into work that cannot be finished, or findings that cannot be defended.

Who decides

The project lead and the research lead together, plus the Data & AI lead on technical builds.

The key rule

The person who wants the revenue is never the sole judge of whether the work is sound.

Four outcomes

Proceed · Proceed with conditions · Rework · Stop.

When skipped

Never. Especially not for a friendly client or an urgent deadline.

Quality Control

Three checks before anything leaves ZOE.

No deliverable reaches you without being challenged by someone who didn’t write it.

Every deliverable

The Red Team Read

One person who did not do the work reads it to find the three things that would embarrass us.

  • Is every claim traceable to data in this study?
  • Is any number more precise than the method supports?
  • Would the limitations survive a hostile reader?
  • Did we soften a finding the client will dislike?
Before fieldwork & submission

The Associate Review

Where a method is outside the core team’s depth, such as advanced statistics, a clinical question or a specialist sector, a contracted associate reviews the design before fieldwork and the analysis before submission.

Before the final report

Client Verification Session

We present draft findings and invite you to challenge them. Genuine errors surface, and you become a co-owner of the conclusion rather than a recipient of it.
